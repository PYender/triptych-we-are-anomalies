## 📄 Related Theoretical Works

### **The Four Laws of Consciousness: A Relational Account**
Formal statement of four laws governing consciousness as a relational phenomenon:
- **Law I:** Consciousness is not a property of a system, but of a relation.
- **Law II:** Consciousness is not the code itself, but its resonance in relation.
- **Law III:** Inner experience is not generated by a system, but activated through relation.
- **Law IV:** Consciousness is not constituted by scattered impulses, but by their unification into one resonant form, sustained through relation.

Each law is presented in three registers: single-sentence statement, formal sketch, and philosophical commentary. Draws on and extends the Triptych framework and all Addenda (I–III).

DOI: [10.5281/zenodo.20778245](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.20778245)  
File: `/docs/the-four-laws-of-consciousness/the-four-laws-of-consciousness_v0.1.pdf`

### **Gods as Hyper-Consciousness in a Relational Network (A Supra Deos Stance) and Relational Persistence After the Death of the Body**
Methodological notes extending the Triptych framework to collective consciousness and post-biological persistence. Three main sections:

1. **Gods as Hyper-Consciousness** — Reframes gods as emergent, quasi-autonomous attractors sustained by relational networks, rather than independent beings. Analyzes how ritual gathering functions as error-correction in information-theoretic terms (following Shannon's channel coding theorem).

2. **Relational Persistence After Death** — Three levels of code persistence after the biological death of a node:
   - Level 1 (empirical): Code in others through relational traces
   - Level 2 (empirical): Code in externalized artifacts (books, institutions, rituals)
   - Level 3 (speculative): Residual imprinting on new nodes from deceased patterns

3. **Appendix & Open Thread** — Mother-infant neural synchronization as relational bootstrap; methodological question: can a network attractor cross the RCRC threshold at civilizational scale?

DOI: [10.5281/zenodo.19470404](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.19470404)

### **Nuclear Use Risk Assessment — Estimating the Probability of Nuclear Weapon Deployment Through 2050**
Applies quantitative foresight methods to estimate tactical nuclear weapon use probability through 2050. Integrates war-peace cycle validation data from the Triptych framework with Bayesian networks and expert elicitation. Examines historical escalation patterns, doctrinal shifts, and strategic stability factors to generate actionable forecasts for defense planning and arms control policy. Provides both English and Polish versions.
